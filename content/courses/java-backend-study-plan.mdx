<Lead>
Reading without building does not survive an interview. Each week below has one reading track and
one artefact you can show and defend.
</Lead>

## Week 1 — Language semantics

**Read:** object model and dispatch, generics and erasure, equals/hashCode and records.

**Build:** a small value-type library — money, date range, version — with correct `equals`,
`hashCode`, `compareTo`, validation in compact constructors, and tests for symmetry and
transitivity.

**Check yourself:** explain why `List<Integer>` is not a `List<Number>`, and why a mutable map key
disappears.

## Week 2 — Collections and strings

**Read:** HashMap internals, choosing a collection, immutability and strings.

**Build:** a bounded LRU cache on `LinkedHashMap`, plus a benchmark comparing `ArrayList` and
`LinkedList` for iteration and middle inserts. Write down the numbers.

**Check yourself:** derive HashMap's default capacity and load factor behaviour from first
principles, then explain treeification thresholds.

## Week 3 — Concurrency

**Read:** race conditions and locks, threads and executors.

**Build:** a rate limiter, first with `synchronized`, then with a `Semaphore`, then with atomics.
Write a test that fails without synchronisation and passes with it.

**Check yourself:** define happens-before without hand-waving, and explain why a virtual thread does
not make CPU-bound code faster.

## Week 4 — JVM and performance

**Read:** class loading and bytecode, garbage collection in practice.

**Build:** a service that allocates deliberately, run it under `-Xlog:gc*`, capture a heap dump with
`jcmd`, and write a one-page diagnosis of what you saw.

**Check yourself:** read a GC log line out loud and say which number indicates a leak.

## Week 5 — Spring Boot and SQL

**Read:** the request lifecycle, indexes and query plans.

**Build:** a REST endpoint with validation, a `@ControllerAdvice`, a transactional service, and one
query that you tune with `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` before and after adding an index.

**Check yourself:** explain why self-invocation bypasses `@Transactional`, and why a function call
around a column prevents index use.

## Week 6 — Reliability and interviews

**Read:** idempotency and retries, the interview bank, the revision sheet.

**Build:** add an idempotency key to the endpoint from week 5, including the unique constraint, the
stored response and a concurrent-duplicate test.

**Check yourself:** answer twenty questions from the bank out loud, in under two minutes each.

## How to study

- Read one chapter, then close it and write the mechanism from memory. The gap is what to re-read.
- Every claim you make in an interview should have a debugging story behind it.
- Prefer one artefact you can explain deeply over five tutorials you copied.
- Keep a running list of "things I assumed and was wrong about" — that list is your revision sheet.

<KeyPoints>
- Pair every reading block with a small artefact you can defend.
- Measure: benchmarks, GC logs, query plans. Numbers beat intuition.
- Explain mechanisms out loud; recognition is not recall.
- Revisit the revision sheet weekly rather than cramming before the interview.
</KeyPoints>
